You Are Beloved

A letter shared from people who follow Jesus · written in his name

Dear friend,

This letter comes from the people of St. James — but the invitation doesn't begin with us. It comes from Jesus of Nazareth, who saw the people that faith had turned away, sat down among them, and said: come.

The Spirit and the bride say, "Come." And let the one who hears say, "Come." Let the one who is thirsty come; and let the one who wishes take the free gift of the water of life.
Revelation 22:17

Here is what we are inviting you to do:

Bring what is on your heart — a prayer, a longing, a grief, a praise, a physical act married to spiritual intention — and entrust it to God. Write it. Tell him what matters to you.

On Sunday, June 21, your prayer will be carried to God's table and lifted before him through the sharing of bread and wine — his true presence, as he commanded. We hold what you entrust with compassion, dignity, and hope. Afterward, we join for a brief ceremony of release: incense, water, and fire. What you gave to the urn is returned to God, and the ashes are kept reverently.

Incense rising before God
Bring all your worries and concerns to God in prayer. And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will keep your hearts and minds in Christ Jesus.
Philippians 4:6-7

If the Church has wounded you, we are sorry. If you were told there is no room for you — that was not the Jesus who sought out the ones religion had left behind. We confess that openly. And here is what we know: the Church is incomplete without you. Your presence, your voice, your prayers — these things the Body of Christ has been missing.

Christ and the Woman at the Well · Carl Heinrich Bloch, 1865–79

You do not need to have faith figured out to come. You do not need to be whole or healed or certain. Bring what you have. Bring your questions. Bring your doubt. Bring what is true for you. Jesus says it is enough.

Come and sit with God. Come and meet a community that believes God still listens. Come and sit with Jesus, and with us, and know that you are beloved.

What Happens To Your Prayer

You are not entering a process. You are being held in prayer.

At Pride
Come As You Are
Today we ask you to place what is on your heart into words and entrust it to the urn. Prayer, grief, gratitude, longing, a quiet cry for help. You do not have to explain your heart here. In words or phrases, a sentence or a paragraph — simply bear your heart to the Lord. Your prayer rests in the urn until worship.
In Worship
Carried To The Table
Your prayer is carried into our next worship service. As we gather at the table of communion, we lift these prayers before the Lord without naming names, honoring your privacy with tenderness and care. Even if no one else knows your story, Jesus does. In his presence, you are held with compassion, dignity, and hope.
After Worship
Released Before The Lord
After worship, you are invited to sit with our community for a brief release rite. We gather with incense, water, and your written prayers, hold a moment of open prayer, and then release these prayers through fire to the Lord. Incense symbolizes prayer rising before God, water recalls baptism, and fire becomes the physical release of what was entrusted. After the ceremony, the ashes are returned to the urn and stored reverently.
